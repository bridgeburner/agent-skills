# Semantic boundaries

Read this reference when a change accepts a new value, collapses several
states, moves a semantic ruling, or adds ordering or attribution metadata.
Skip it when the representation and all meanings are unchanged.

- Exercise each behaviorally distinct input alone and beside the relevant
  recognized state at the same identity, window, or key. Record the accepted,
  rejected, warned, quarantined, or normalized result, stored form, failure
  radius, and relevant drop, omission, quarantine, or wrong-attribution signal.
- Identify the first resolver and every later loader, re-reader, duplicate
  resolver, ledger, API/UI, and terminal consumer. A later component is a
  second authority until it reuses the canonical ruling or proves equivalence.
  A declared out-of-scope consumer does not close an outcome contradiction.
- Treat provenance and source text as descriptive. They do not authorize an
  accept, reject, quarantine, or fail-open decision unless a typed capability
  owns that decision and callers use its serialized form consistently.
- An ordinal orders only its declared batch, request, file, or run namespace.
  Cross-namespace comparison needs a cross-scope key or a proven single-
  namespace invariant.
- Match proof to the claimed boundary. An integrated behavior claim needs a
  terminal product-path assertion and its relevant negative sinks; source,
  focused tests, or artifacts can support the claim but cannot silently replace
  missing terminal evidence.
